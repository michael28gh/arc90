THE RECOMMENDATION

Arc90 should become the app that turns your real-life constraints into a manageable day, then connects those actions to a meaningful 90-day outcome.

Not another place to collect numbers. Not an AI that invents a supplement regimen. A small, reliable action system: understand today, choose the next step, record it once, learn over time.

Proposed product promise: A day you can keep. A change you can see.
Positioning hypothesis, not a proven competitive advantage or a medical outcome claim.

AI health coaches, readiness scores, supplement lists, streaks, and correlations are established features. Bevel even lists calendar integrations. WHOOP added custom journal behaviors on September 18, 2026.

Where we can compete

Execution clarity. One next action, with the reason it fits today. Fewer decisions and no dashboard homework.

Trustworthy records. A dose log means exactly what happened, not an inferred symptom report or a resettable daily checkbox.

Personal continuity. Turn recurring obstacles, weekly reviews, and actual goal outcomes into a plan that gets easier to use.

These are opportunities to validate. This research does not establish that no competitor offers a similar workflow.

First: repair Protocol's record integrity

The current Protocol view is a lightweight daily checklist, not yet a dependable multi-dose tracker.

  • Weekly and as-needed items enter the same daily denominator as daily items.
  • A morning entry followed by an evening entry leaves only the evening entry.
  • A quick check-in writes a "no symptoms" value the user did not report.
  • Adding a new item today can lower yesterday's percentage.

These are code-backed findings, not design preferences. No app fixes were applied during this audit.

01 / THE FLAGSHIP

A day that actually fits

Upgrade Adaptive Day from a mode switch into a small, capacity-aware plan. The app should offer the next useful action you can realistically do, not eight obligations that ignore the rest of your life.

Illustrative UI: "You have 25 minutes free. One study block moves this week's milestone forward." Start / Adjust
Presentation
One compact row inside Today's Arc. Tap for two alternatives and a short "Why this?" explanation. Replanning replaces this row; it does not add a new dashboard.
Variables
Available minutes, user energy check-in, goal priority, minimum/full habit duration, actual session duration, deadlines, recent completion windows, data freshness.
Connections
Optional read-only calendar access; existing Focus timer; Apple Health for optional context. Manual availability remains fully usable without a wearable.
What is genuinely new here
Arc90 already has modes, capacity selection and best-window logic. Add structured durations and conflict-aware planning tied to the user's 90-day milestone. Never silently change medication timing or amounts.
Competition reality
Sunsama already timeboxes work; RISE forecasts energy; Bevel has calendar integrations. Our hypothesis is that a smaller, goal-linked daily interaction earns preference, not that calendar plus health is novel.
First version / test
Manual availability plus one milestone, no predictive AI. Measure time to first meaningful action, plan acceptance and completion, and whether users feel the plan was realistic. Add calendar access only after the manual workflow helps.

02 / PROTOCOL DIFFERENTIATION

A Protocol Passport, not a bigger stack

Give every item a reliable identity: what it is, why the user tracks it, whose instructions were recorded, what was actually logged and what is still unknown. Keep the daily screen extremely simple.

Illustrative UI: "Morning · 2 scheduled items" with product name, recorded amount, time and a clear Taken action. Source and history are one tap deeper.
Presentation
Now / Later / Logged sections, followed by a collapsed Library. Product detail opens a sheet with Plan, History and Evidence. No giant percentage ring or confetti for consuming substances.
Variables
Product and ingredient identity, amount plus unit, label serving versus recorded regimen, schedule version, scheduled/actual time, source, optional stock count, batch/expiry and review date.
Connections
Begin with manual entry and a user-reviewed label photo. Later, optionally read authorized Apple Health medication events and match them without duplicate logging. The current Arc90 bridge does not implement these medication APIs.
What is genuinely new here
Replace date-only toggles with occurrence-level events and provenance. Add an honest record of plan changes and a readable export. Inventory is secondary, never an incentive to finish a container or reorder.
Competition reality
Apple, Medisafe, SuppCo and specialist peptide trackers already cover much of this territory. Arc90's opportunity is to connect reliable records to the whole day and goal review while staying less demanding to use.
First version / test
Schedule engine, editable history, undo, clear status, source labels and export first. Test wrong-day logging, duplicate taps, corrections and a two-occurrence day. Dose-error rate must be zero in deterministic tests before health insights depend on these records.

Apple's read medication and dose-event APIsApple MedicationsSuppCo

Peptides are not one interchangeable category. Do not assign a generic once-daily plan, dose, cycle, or reconstitution instruction. Record the person's existing professional instructions. Evidence cards need a dated, jurisdiction-specific source and must distinguish product approval, compounding status, research evidence and quality. They are different questions. FDA safety context

03 / LEARNING WITHOUT MORE LOGGING

One question at a time

A guided personal learning loop: pick a low-risk behavior and one outcome that matters to the goal, reuse existing records, then show what the data can and cannot tell us.

Illustrative question: "Do my phone-free study blocks lead to more practice questions completed?" Not: "This supplement caused a 20% recovery boost."
Presentation
One active question in Arc, with a small Today action when relevant. Review shows raw observations, coverage and competing explanations before a keep / revise / stop decision.
Variables
Behavior timestamps, goal outcome, weekday, workload, sleep coverage, missing entries, disruptions and plan changes. Separate not logged from no; preselect same-day or next-day outcomes.
Connections
Focus session records, habit events, optional HealthKit context and one user-chosen outcome metric. No request to fill out a 30-question daily form.
What is genuinely new here
Arc90 has descriptive patterns, not a complete question-to-action-to-review workflow with comparable baseline periods. Build that workflow before a large correlation engine.
Competition reality
Bearable already offers experiments; WHOOP, Oura, Bevel and Guava surface associations. The difference to test is lower effort and a direct link to the chosen 90-day outcome. Do not market causality from observational data.
First version / test
One behavioral question, one outcome, a descriptive review. Show "not enough comparable data" when appropriate. Validate analysis with statistical expertise before publishing confidence claims. Measure review usefulness and decisions retained at follow-up, not the number of insights generated.

Bearable experimentsGuava correlations

Do not randomize, skip, start or change medications, peptides or prescribed regimens as an app experiment. Protocol observations can be exported for clinician review, without treatment recommendations.

04 / REMEMBER WHAT MAKES THINGS EASIER

A personal friction playbook

Make the app remember why a plan became difficult and which practical adjustment the user chose. Future difficult days should require less explaining, not a longer conversation with an AI coach.

Illustrative UI: "Last time work ran late, you chose a 5-minute review. Use that version today?" Use / Change
Presentation
Offer Time / Energy / Distracted / Unclear after a user asks for help. Store a short, editable if-then plan. Optional journal extraction proposes a memory for approval; private writing is never silently turned into a profile. Prefer on-device processing and require explicit consent before any cloud analysis.
Variables
User-selected obstacle, chosen response, context, acceptance, next action and whether it helped. Treat retrospective success as a useful memory, not proof the suggestion caused it.
Connections
Existing journal and comeback tools, optional calendar busy time, Focus and a future Watch action. No inferred location or employer monitoring.
What is genuinely new here
Arc90 already provides comeback micro-actions and recovery rate. Add context-specific memory and feedback, with view/edit/delete controls. Do not add another generic comeback card or shame-based notification.
Competition reality
Gentler Streak already treats activity and recovery flexibly, and AI coaches have memory. The opportunity is a transparent, user-owned library of working responses tied to actual obstacles.
First version / test
Explicit if-then rules work without an LLM. Measure accepted useful responses, time to return after a disrupted day, notification dismissal and perceived guilt. A smaller target must not be counted as the original full target.

05 / THE REASON TO STAY FOR 90 DAYS

An outcome-linked Arc

Separate doing the work from achieving the goal. An attractive 90-day grid shows consistency; it does not prove a user learned more, saved money, improved a skill or finished a project.

Illustrative weekly review: "You kept 5 study sessions. Your recorded practice score moved from 62 to 70. What should we keep next week?" No causal claim.
Presentation
Keep the Arc as the visual identity. Add a restrained outcome line and milestone markers with attached evidence. Tap a milestone for a session, note or artifact. A weekly review replaces several disconnected charts.
Variables
One outcome definition, baseline, unit, direction, review cadence, milestone target, proof source, actions and actual results. Keep missing measurements visibly missing.
Connections
Link existing Proof Wall notes/photos and Focus sessions. Start with manual goal results; defer bank, employer and medical-record integrations. Share only items the user explicitly selects.
What is genuinely new here
Proof Wall and the 90-day grid already exist. Add a real relation between each proof item, milestone and goal measure. Never manufacture a successful outcome from completed checkboxes.
Competition reality
Streaks tracks habits and statistics; health tools track health trends; work tools track tasks. A cross-domain, evidence-backed 90-day review is a positioning opportunity, not a verified exclusive feature.
First version / test
One goal metric, three milestones and weekly evidence selection. Measure useful weekly reviews, repeat goal-setting and outcome follow-up. Never rank users by medication adherence or expose health records on a public share card.

Protocol audit: ten improvements

Reviewed the local v139 Protocol screen at a phone-sized viewport, followed the primary add/detail flow, inspected the data model and event handlers, and ran isolated tests against the actual source functions. No real health records were modified; UI testing used the separate localhost QA profile.

P0: record integrity P1: usable core workflow P2: differentiation

1. Show what is due, not everything in the library P0

Finding: Daily, Weekly and As needed all enter today's total. The interface can suggest that an optional item is an unfinished obligation.

Improve: Generate scheduled occurrences from weekdays, intervals, times and effective dates. Keep optional use in a separate group with no missed-dose penalty. Distinguish upcoming, due, recorded skipped, taken and unlogged; unlogged does not prove not taken.

Test: One logged daily item plus one weekly item not due and one as-needed item should show 1/1 scheduled, not 1/3. Current source reproduces the 1/3 result.

js/app.js:2042, js/app.js:7317, js/app.js:7387. Benchmark: Apple Medications scheduling

2. Make day and night separate records P0

Finding: "Day + night" is a display category, but upsertProtocolLog removes previous logs for the same date. Morning and evening are not independently retained.

Improve: Store an occurrence ID, plan-version ID, scheduled time, actual time, timezone, amount/unit snapshot, source and status. The visible checklist represents occurrences, not just products.

Test: Record two scheduled occurrences on the same day; both must remain. Test repeated taps, midnight, daylight saving, timezone travel and offline reconciliation. Current source retains only the second entry.

js/app.js:2082, js/app.js:7408, js/app.js:9133. Benchmark: Peptide Tracker's occurrence-level workflow

3. Separate intake, symptoms and corrections P0

Finding: Quick logging writes symptoms: ['none'] without asking. Tapping again deletes all of that day's entries, including any detailed symptom notes. The signal text can then imply reassurance.

Improve: Intake logs must not create symptom claims. Default to "Not assessed." Make symptom logging optional and separately timestamped. Provide a visible Undo for the specific action and a correction history. Corrections and user-requested deletion are different operations.

Test: A quick log cannot report "feeling fine." Undoing it must preserve separate symptom entries. A recorded severe symptom must remain visible after an intake correction.

js/app.js:2060, js/app.js:9126, js/app.js:9141. No clinician assessment or emergency-triage validation was performed.

4. Make reminders real and inspectable P1

Finding: The form says "Reminder time," but no per-protocol scheduling consumer was found. App reminders operate on the global habit schedule and tasks, not each protocol's time.

Improve: Show permission status, next scheduled reminder and a test action. Use supported native notifications for offline iPhone delivery; keep web support honestly labeled. Snooze, cancellation, plan edits and timezone changes must update the schedule without duplicates.

Test: Foreground/background, app closed, denied permission, reboot, offline, travel and an edited plan on a real iPhone. Until implemented, rename the field "Planned time" and do not promise a notification.

js/app.js:7533, js/app.js:9106, checkReminders(), syncPushSubscription(). This is a source finding, not a physical-device notification test.

5. Use a quick setup review, then allow editing and archiving P1

Finding: Tapping a template immediately creates an item with generic defaults. The main detail view is read-only. The add form's "close" control sets protoAddOpen = true, so it stays open; this was reproduced in the UI.

Improve: Template tap opens a short review: identity, existing instructions, schedule, start date, reminder choice. Offer Edit, Pause in Arc90, Archive and Undo. Clarify that pausing tracking is not medical advice to stop treatment. Preserve history and detect duplicates.

Test: Cancel makes no changes; Close closes; a plan edit affects future occurrences only; archive preserves old entries. Warn before destructive deletion and offer export.

js/app.js:9071, js/app.js:9073, js/app.js:7435, js/app.js:9125.

6. Replace the dashboard with a calm daily list P1

Finding: The empty screen offers 12 starter tiles, another template entry, a percentage, manual add, export and a full sleep module. After setup it repeats the count in a "Signal" paragraph. Category emoji and card nesting do not match the calmer Today design.

Improve: Date + Add at the top. Compact Now / Later / Logged rows, with product, recorded amount, time and status. Collapse the Library below the active plan. Keep Sleep in its own Lab destination; link a small relevant signal instead of embedding the entire module.

Visual spec: Approximately 64-72px rows with wrapping, 44px minimum interactive targets, 16-18px library icons, 8px or smaller corners, neutral surfaces, sage for recorded completion, amber only for actionable uncertainty. A short check transition and optional haptic, with reduced-motion support. No color-only status.

js/app.js:6977, js/app.js:7317, js/app.js:7352. Design judgment based on the observed current phone-sized layout.

7. Show an honest history, not a misleading pulse P0

Finding: Historical percentages divide by today's item count. An isolated test changed yesterday from 100% to 50% by adding an unlogged item today. Zero-value bars still have an 8% height.

Improve: A 7/30-day dose timeline using the plan active on each date. Distinguish taken, recorded skipped, unlogged and not scheduled. Zero means zero; unavailable history is not failure. Optional usage sits outside scheduled adherence.

Test: Add, edit and archive today without rescoring the past. Expose dates and counts to screen readers, plus a text list beneath the chart. Describe this as recorded adherence, not proof of consumption or effectiveness.

js/app.js:2087, js/app.js:7369. Benchmark: Forward-only schedule edits

8. Build an evidence-aware library, not a "popular stack" P2

Finding: A generic peptide template defaults to a daily morning plan. Category names and free-text amounts do not identify exact products or ingredient overlap.

Improve: Search by ingredient, exact product or a custom recorded plan. For vitamins/supplements, separate label serving from the user's entered amount and support reviewed ingredient totals. For peptides and medications, record professional instructions without guessing frequency or dose. Keep regulatory status, clinical evidence and product quality separate, with sources and review dates.

Boundary: Start with a small, reviewed library. A duplicate-ingredient flag is not a comprehensive interaction check. Never display "safe together" based on an incomplete database; use clinician/pharmacist review for medication decisions.

js/data.js:356, js/app.js:7510. NIH supplement labels, combinations and recordkeepingFDA compounding safety context

9. Record once across Today, Protocol and Apple devices P2

Finding: Protocol water/sleep routines and the actual health counters can describe the same activity without a shared event. Watch source displays the next protocol, but its protocol action requests a snapshot rather than logging a dose.

Improve: Give linked activities one source event and origin. A completed water goal can reference the glass counter without inventing extra water. Later, support explicit Watch actions and authorized HealthKit medication reads with deduplication and visible sync status.

Test: Two offline devices replaying the same event must not double the count. Handle edited and deleted imported events. Never silently merge substances by similar names. No direct Oura/WHOOP connection is claimed here.

js/app.js:9299, ios/Watch/Arc90WatchApp.swift:227, ios/App/App/Arc90HealthPlugin.swift:39. Apple medication read authorization and event updates

10. Make review and export genuinely useful P1

Finding: The text export says it contains no dosing information, then includes the recorded dose/amount. It lacks occurrence times and plan-version history. The primary Protocol details do not expose the legacy symptom-entry flow. Several form labels are not associated with inputs, and selection state is not expressed on the quick-log buttons.

Improve: A readable detail/history sheet with accessible labels and states. Offer date-filtered CSV and a clear print/PDF report with an export preview, chosen fields, actual timestamps, units, sources, corrections, symptom entries and missing-data labels. Keep basic export free.

Test: Screen-reader and large-text flows; correct local dates; export round-trip; old logs without times must remain "time unknown." Health details must be excluded from social sharing unless explicitly selected. A report records observations; it does not diagnose.

What users might pay for

Propose charging for useful synthesis: deeper goal planning, multi-goal reviews and optional rich integrations. Keep reliable basic logs, correction, data portability and essential safety information available without payment.

Bevel's free feature breadth and Bearable's free tracking/export raise the bar for a subscription based only on reminders and charts.

Bevel access modelBearable tiers

Do not pick a price before testing repeated value and willingness to pay. Geographic offers and checkout prices were not exhaustively compared.

What not to build first

  • A giant supplement marketplace or affiliate-driven stack recommendations.
  • Automated peptide dosing, titration, reconstitution or medical treatment decisions.
  • A new composite score without an understandable decision attached.
  • A social leaderboard based on health records or substance consumption.
  • A chat box as the primary interface for tasks that need one tap.
  • More animated cards that make the daily screen longer.

Validate with actual users next

This is desk research plus a hands-on audit of our app, not completed field interviews. Recruit a small directional sample of busy goal-setters, people already tracking clinician-directed routines and wearable users. Include people without a wearable and users of larger text.

  1. Ask them to plan a disrupted day, log a scheduled occurrence, correct a mistake and find last week's history. Observe without explaining.
  2. Compare the current and proposed Prototype flows in alternating order. Measure task success, time, wrong-item taps and confidence in the record.
  3. Run a short diary pilot with voluntary, minimal data collection. Track daily effort, useful next actions and whether the weekly review changes a plan.
  4. Ask what they would replace, what they would keep alongside Arc90 and whether the proposed paid value matters. Do not extrapolate a small qualitative sample into market share.

Working success targets, not observed results: daily logging in a few taps; no record corruption in the test matrix; lower time-to-action; fewer dismissals; a weekly review users can explain in their own words. Health claims need separate clinical and regulatory assessment.

What was verified

  • Arc90 local build v139, source-level Protocol behavior, phone-sized browser layout and the template/manual-add path.
  • Isolated source-function reproductions: daily denominator 1/3, two same-day logs reduced to one, historical score 100% to 50% after adding an item.
  • Primary product/help pages for 15 adjacent products, accessed September 28, 2026. Earlier Protocol inspection was refreshed against the current source.
  • Official Apple developer/support, NIH and FDA references for integration and health-data boundaries.

What was not verified

  • No independent clinical validation, regulatory legal review, representative market survey or completed customer interviews.
  • No paid-account hands-on comparison, physical iPhone/Watch build test, notification delivery test, or proof that any competitor feature improves outcomes.
  • No exhaustive regional pricing study, vendor API partnership check or confirmation that every vendor feature is available to every subscription and country.
  • No formal WCAG certification. Accessibility findings are targeted source/UI observations; full keyboard, screen-reader and native Dynamic Type testing remains necessary.

Local model/code presence is not the same as successful native deployment. Competitor documentation is first-party product evidence and may contain marketing claims; it is not neutral proof of performance. A feature not found in these pages is unknown, not absent.

Decision rule

Do not claim "we beat everyone." Aim to win a specific repeated job: helping a busy person follow a realistic plan, maintain trustworthy records and see meaningful 90-day progress with less daily effort. Reliability is the entry requirement. The integrated workflow is the differentiation hypothesis.
