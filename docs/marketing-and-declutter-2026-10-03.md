# Arc90: Make The Preview Worth Asking For

Draft strategy and source-based design audit, October 3, 2026. No app code, deployments, social accounts, automations, messages, or spending were changed. Claude's implementation work remains separate.

## The Positioning

**Arc90 helps you choose the next move toward a goal, even when the day changes.**

Creative territory: real days, meaningful goals, imperfect follow-through. Start with people studying around work, a reachable audience hypothesis that fits the founder's existing story. Do not try to market simultaneously as a clinical tracker, calendar, AI coach, productivity game, and social network.

The distinctive offer is not access to another dashboard. It is a small experience that makes someone's next decision easier. Treat these ideas as hypotheses to test, not a claim that no other app has tried them.

## The Comment-To-Preview Campaign

Start on Instagram with one post-specific keyword, one promise, and one destination.

Draft CTA: **"Comment ARC and I'll send you the interactive preview. Try a real day before you sign up."**

Only use the no-signup sentence once the public preview genuinely supports it. The current purpose-review experiment is local-only and is not a valid public destination.

Proposed path:

1. A short video shows one recognizable problem and one working interaction.
2. A viewer comments ARC to request the link. Include a bio-link alternative for people who prefer not to comment publicly.
3. Deliver one requested DM with the preview link, without requiring a follow, an email, or another comment first.
4. The preview opens directly into the scenario shown in the video, using labeled example data.
5. After the useful interaction, offer optional beta signup with clear consent. Keep the preview usable without subscribing.

Draft DM, for use only once the destination works:

> Here's the Arc90 preview you asked for: [PUBLIC PREVIEW LINK]. It uses example data. No account needed to try it.

Optional question in that same message: "What felt confusing?" Do not automatically send reminders just because someone requested the link.

Manychat documents Instagram comment-to-DM link delivery. Its documentation distinguishes a requested private reply from permission for subsequent automated messages; an external website-link click alone does not open the messaging window. Configure only the promised delivery initially, verify the account's capabilities, and test with a non-owner account. This does not establish equivalent TikTok functionality. [Comment trigger documentation](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/14281316989724-Instagram-Post-and-Reel-Comments-trigger), [link automation documentation](https://help.manychat.com/hc/en-us/articles/16654065283100-Quick-Automation-Auto-DM-links-from-comments).

No bought comments, fake testers, fabricated results, automated bulk outreach, or false scarcity. TikTok's guidelines prohibit deceptive engagement and spam; do not turn the concept into like-for-like or misleading incentives. [TikTok integrity guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity/).

## Eight Campaigns With A Product Experience Behind Them

### 1. Borrow My Tuesday

Hook: "Before you download another productivity app, borrow my Tuesday."

Show work, study, and a personal goal colliding. The visitor receives an interactive example: a late finish leaves 15 minutes, and they choose an explicitly smaller action. Their choice updates the sample plan, not their real calendar.

Why test it: demonstrates the product's judgment and control, not only its appearance. Deliverable: a single scenario with two meaningful choices. No general AI required. This scenario is proposed, not built in the existing purpose-review preview.

### 2. The 6:47 PM Test

Hook: "Your day went wrong. You still care about the goal. What now?"

Every episode starts at the same time with a different ordinary interruption. Show the user's decision and the actual screen response. Turn the time stamp into a recognizable series device, not a countdown or urgency trick.

CTA: comment ARC for that episode's scenario. The episode can be staged if labeled as an example; don't present a scripted event as a customer testimonial.

### 3. Retire One Goal, Rescue One

Hook: "You don't need to restart every promise you made in January."

Invite people to choose one old intention they still care about and deliberately archive one they don't. The private preview turns the retained goal into a next action. Never require people to post private notes or health/financial goals in public comments.

Format: anonymous, permission-based submissions or clearly invented sample notes. A printable worksheet can test the idea before a dedicated product workflow exists.

### 4. The Day-18 Diaries

Hook: "Everyone shows day one. I'm showing the middle."

Follow Michael and a small consenting beta group after the initial excitement. Show missed actions, edits to the plan, and actual next-day decisions. Eighteen is a creative series name, not a scientific claim about when motivation fails.

The interesting episode is a useful adjustment, not a perfect streak. Avoid staged before-and-after results or a promised transformation in 90 days.

### 5. The Delete Club

Hook: "Help me remove one thing from my app."

Show two real interface options and ask what people would stop displaying on the first screen. Publish the result and explain what was kept accessible elsewhere. Then let viewers test the revised click path.

This joins marketing and decluttering in one honest build-in-public series. Treat comments as qualitative feedback, not representative user research or a binding vote to remove someone else's essential feature.

### 6. Three People, One Goal

Use one goal with a student, a working parent, and someone with a changing schedule. Each has a different plan and chooses a different next step. The goal matters more than copying an influencer's entire routine.

Begin with labeled personas unless real participants consent. Collaborate with creators on a small, transferable plan template; templates must not leak their journals or imply sponsorship unless it exists.

### 7. Your Next 90 Starts Before Something

Anchor a chapter to a real upcoming moment: an exam, a portfolio review, or a self-chosen project deadline. Record a baseline and a private note about what matters. A later recap compares the plan with actual evidence.

This is stronger than a generic New Year challenge because the event supplies context. It must not promise passing an exam or guarantee outcomes. Voice/time-capsule functionality remains a proposal.

### 8. A Receipt For The Week

Create a small, optional share artifact: what I intended, what I actually did, and one adjustment. No ranking, invented achievement, default public journal, or giant score.

A recipient can ask for the underlying plan, stripped of personal records. Prototype the share format manually before building referral plumbing. It can become a recognizable visual signature without adding another daily dashboard card.

## Creative Direction And Formats

- Instagram Reel: 15-25 seconds, a recognizable moment, one screen interaction, one CTA. Cover titles should name the situation, not a feature list.
- Instagram carousel: the old plan, the disruption, the smaller plan, and an invitation to try it. Put the complete useful idea in the carousel; don't withhold all value behind a comment.
- TikTok: founder-led narrative with the real app on screen. Use a tested native contact/bio path rather than promising Instagram-style automation without verification. Commercial disclosures should match the platform requirements and actual relationship.
- Stories: compare two versions of a screen, then show the implemented result. Do not use polls as fake evidence of effectiveness.
- Visual identity: actual, legible screens; daylight or ordinary work settings; consistent framing; one useful motion. Avoid an endless stream of black phone renders, stock success scenes, and unsupported AI testimonials.

Twenty-second draft:

0-3s: "My plan looked great this morning. Then the day happened."
3-8s: show the original commitment and the interruption.
8-15s: show the user choosing a smaller action connected to the same goal.
15-20s: "I'm building Arc90 for days like this. Comment ARC for the preview."

Record only a verified working flow. Otherwise visibly label it a concept prototype.

## Test Plan

First pass: three creative angles, same delivery promise and same preview. Suggested angles: Borrow My Tuesday, The 6:47 PM Test, The Delete Club. Use an initial seven-day observation period, then wait long enough to measure week-later return among actual beta entrants. This is directional learning, not a statistically definitive experiment.

Track the steps separately: relevant comments, successfully delivered DMs, unique preview opens where measurable, completed preview scenario, optional signup, and meaningful return. Use aggregate campaign IDs, not journal content. Do not report a DM link click as a product signup. App analytics and campaign tagging are not implemented by this document.

Primary early question: **Did a person understand the value and finish the useful interaction?** A thousand comments with no useful preview actions is not success. If clicks are high but interaction completion is low, fix the preview before producing more ads.

Public-preview gate: approved separate hosting, correct mobile loading, labeled sample data, no production credentials/account mutation, no unexpected sign-in wall, a tested DM destination, and explicit permission before beta marketing messages. Nothing is scheduled or published here.

### Improvements Already Present: Keep Them

Water and mood are already preference-controlled in `todaySignalsPanel`. Six additional charts are already behind More diagrams. Completed/unscheduled habit sections already use disclosure. Current Today no longer renders every old historical panel found elsewhere in the file. Do not "fix" obsolete or inactive components as if they were all still on the home screen.

The proposed principle is progressive disclosure: keep the primary work visible and move secondary detail to a predictable location, without deleting capability. [Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/).

## Proposed Screen Jobs

- **Today:** choose and perform the next action. Show the goal it supports, Start/Complete, the remaining selected actions, and a compact optional check-in. Keep completed work accessible and offer a user-controlled hide-completed mode rather than making rows disappear mid-task.
- **Arc:** organize goals, their relationships, tasks, habits and journal history. Keep one creation entry point with a review step; capturing text never silently creates obligations.
- **Progress:** understand what changed. Separate actual outcomes from consistency and show one finding with evidence and a user-chosen adjustment. Keep the chart library available.
- **Lab:** optional focus/health tools and records, not a second home dashboard. Preserve direct shortcuts for frequently used tools and place Lab next to You as requested.
- **You:** account, preferences, data controls and support. No repeated launch-newsletter form in the daily workflow.
